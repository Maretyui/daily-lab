Scroll-Driven, Zero-JS

Your browser doesn't support CSS scroll-driven animations — this demo is falling back to a static, always-visible layout instead of silently breaking.

The bar at the very top of the page is a plain <div> scaled with a normal @keyframes animation — except its timeline isn't time, it's animation-timeline: scroll(root block), so scroll position drives the 0%–100% keyframe progress directly instead of a scroll event listener computing scrollY / scrollHeight on every frame.

Section one

Each section below fades and slides in via animation-timeline: view(), a per-element timeline tied to how far that element has scrolled through the viewport — no IntersectionObserver in sight. animation-range: entry 0% cover 30% scopes the keyframe to just the entry transition.

Section two

Both timelines are driven entirely by the compositor thread, so scrolling stays smooth even on a heavy page — there's no main-thread JS recalculating styles per scroll tick at all.

Section three

Keep scrolling — the top bar keeps tracking overall progress while each section re-plays its own entry animation independently the moment it comes into view.

Section four

Support: Chromium has shipped this since 2023; Firefox and Safari are following via the same spec. The @supports not (animation-timeline: scroll()) block above swaps in a static fallback rather than leaving unsupported browsers looking broken.
