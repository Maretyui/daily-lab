137 — Langton's Ant

One ant, one rule, a blank grid: on a white cell turn right and flip it black, on a black cell turn left and flip it white, then step forward. A few hundred steps look like noise — then, every single time, the ant breaks into a diagonal "highway" that marches off forever. Tap the canvas or the button to restart with a fresh seed and watch it happen again.

The grid is a plain array of 0/1 cells, each pixel a cell. The ant keeps an (x, y) position and a heading (up/right/down/left). Every step it reads the cell it's standing on, turns 90° left or right depending on that single bit, flips the bit, and moves forward one cell in its new heading. That's the entire rule — no randomness, no memory beyond the grid itself — yet after roughly 10,000 steps it reliably self-organizes into an open-ended diagonal path. Restarting reseeds a small patch of random noise under the ant's start point so the early chaotic phase looks a little different each run.
